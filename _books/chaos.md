James Gleick tells the story of how chaos theory took shape between the 1960s and 1980s, as researchers in meteorology, physics, biology, and mathematics found that simple deterministic systems can behave unpredictably. The book follows Edward Lorenz's weather simulations and the "butterfly effect," Benoit Mandelbrot's fractals, and Mitchell Feigenbaum's discovery of universal constants in period-doubling, among others. It's part history and part science writing, and it shows how a new field formed across traditional disciplinary lines.
