Paul Lockhart, a research mathematician turned K–12 teacher, argues that school mathematics has stripped the subject of what makes it mathematics: curiosity, creativity, and the pleasure of building an argument. He opens with a musician's nightmare, a world where music is taught only as notation and rules, and then turns that critique on the standard math curriculum. The second half sketches what learning math could look like instead, with open-ended problems that students can work out for themselves.

If you have never felt extremist about math education reform, read this and I bet you will!
